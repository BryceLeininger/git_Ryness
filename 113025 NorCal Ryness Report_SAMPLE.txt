A rare pricing flip in the U.S. housing market, something that hasn't happened in over a
half a century, is puzzling analysts, who worry that it might be indictive of deeper
structural issues. Last week, The Kobeissi Letter said, "the US housing market is
broken," while noting that the average prices of new single-family homes have been
lower than that of existing homes over the past year, "the first occurrence in 54 years."
The shift marks a stark reversal from historic trends, where newly built homes have
almost always commanded a premium due to modern construction, updated features
and limited new supply relative to existing inventory. Citing data from Apollo Chief
Economist, the U.S. Census Bureau and the National Association of Realtors, the post
notes that the price of a new home fell below the price of an existing home "for the first
time since 2005." The post also highlights how dramatic the swing has been compared
to recent years. "By comparison, new homes were 10% more expensive just 3 years
ago, measured by the 12-month moving average," it said, adding that "the difference
was as much as 40% in 2012," following the 2008 financial crisis. It concludes by
saying, "Something is seriously wrong with the housing market." Source: Benzinga.com

A rare pricing flip in the U.S. housing market, something that hasn't happened in over a
half a century, is puzzling analysts, who worry that it might be indictive of deeper
structural issues. Last week, The Kobeissi Letter said, "the US housing market is
broken," while noting that the average prices of new single-family homes have been
lower than that of existing homes over the past year, "the first occurrence in 54 years."
The shift marks a stark reversal from historic trends, where newly built homes have
almost always commanded a premium due to modern construction, updated features
and limited new supply relative to existing inventory. Citing data from Apollo Chief
Economist, the U.S. Census Bureau and the National Association of Realtors, the post
notes that the price of a new home fell below the price of an existing home "for the first
time since 2005." The post also highlights how dramatic the swing has been compared
to recent years. "By comparison, new homes were 10% more expensive just 3 years
ago, measured by the 12-month moving average," it said, adding that "the difference
was as much as 40% in 2012," following the 2008 financial crisis. It concludes by
saying, "Something is seriously wrong with the housing market." Source: Benzinga.com

A rare pricing flip in the U.S. housing market, something that hasn't happened in over a
half a century, is puzzling analysts, who worry that it might be indictive of deeper
structural issues. Last week, The Kobeissi Letter said, "the US housing market is
broken," while noting that the average prices of new single-family homes have been
lower than that of existing homes over the past year, "the first occurrence in 54 years."
The shift marks a stark reversal from historic trends, where newly built homes have
almost always commanded a premium due to modern construction, updated features
and limited new supply relative to existing inventory. Citing data from Apollo Chief
Economist, the U.S. Census Bureau and the National Association of Realtors, the post
notes that the price of a new home fell below the price of an existing home "for the first
time since 2005." The post also highlights how dramatic the swing has been compared
to recent years. "By comparison, new homes were 10% more expensive just 3 years
ago, measured by the 12-month moving average," it said, adding that "the difference
was as much as 40% in 2012," following the 2008 financial crisis. It concludes by
saying, "Something is seriously wrong with the housing market." Source: Benzinga.com
